The subsystem screens answer one question each: what is this part of the car doing right now. They
read a fixed set of signals, show the current value of each, and name the module and identifier that
answered.

<SafetyNotice level="read-only">
Every screen on this page is a read. Each signal is fetched with `0x22 ReadDataByIdentifier` and
decoded locally. Nothing here writes to a module, starts a routine, or changes how the car behaves.
</SafetyNotice>

## Beyond live telemetry

Live telemetry is a polling loop over a channel set you choose, and its point is how a signal moves
over time. Subsystem telemetry is the opposite shape: a single pass over a fixed question list, one
current answer per signal. Use the subsystem screens to check whether a value is plausible now. Use
[Live telemetry](/docs/live-telemetry) to watch it change under load. Both read the same car over the
same transport, and both keep the source of every reading.

## How a subsystem read works

Each signal carries its own state: `measured` when the module answered and the payload decoded,
`unsupported` when the module refuses that identifier, `failed` when the read timed out or the
payload was malformed, and `not_attempted` when the platform cannot map the module at all.

Two rules follow:

- A missing or failed reading is shown as missing, never as zero. A zero on these screens is a
  measured zero.
- A decoded value that is physically impossible for its sensor fails closed. It becomes a typed
  failure instead of a number.

The screen shows the source as an address and a DID, so an empty row still tells you what was asked.

## Climate and the heat pump

The climate screen reads thirteen signals from three modules: the VCU1 refrigerant and heater
circuits, the CCM evaporator, and the ECM heat-pump temperatures and radiator fan. They cover the
refrigerant inlet and outlet pressure and temperature, the heater circuit inlet and outlet, the
electric compressor speed, the evaporator core, the compressor inlet, the condenser, the battery
cooling circuit, the heat exchanger, and the radiator fan duty.

Each value is range-gated before it is displayed:

- refrigerant pressure 0 to 40 bar
- circuit and core temperatures −40 to 150 °C, evaporator −40 to 80 °C
- compressor speed 0 to 15000 rpm
- radiator fan duty 0 to 100 %

A normal reading is a compressor turning when cooling is demanded, an evaporator colder than the
cabin, and an inlet-to-outlet pressure difference across the running loop. A suspect reading is a
compressor at zero rpm while cooling is demanded, inlet and outlet pressure sitting on top of each
other with the compressor running, or one temperature failing plausibility while its neighbours on
the same module answer.

The screen draws the loop as a schematic and lists the same thirteen values in a table. The pack
cooling circuit temperature is one row there. The full module temperature map is a battery read, and
its sensor placement is still provisional; see [Battery diagnostics](/docs/battery-diagnostics).

## Drive units

The motors screen reads the front drive unit (IHFA) and the rear drive unit (IEM), nine signals per
axle: stator winding 1 and 2 temperatures, rotor speed, axle torque, inverter HV bus voltage,
coolant inlet and outlet, inverter HV current, and the fuse-side terminal voltage from the BECM.

A normal parked car shows rotor speed near zero, torque near zero, both windings close to the
coolant, and an HV bus voltage near the pack voltage. Under load, torque and current rise together
and the windings run warmer than the coolant.

The screen classifies rotor motion with a near-zero deadband and keeps the raw value on display next
to it. A small parked reading, such as −7.3 rpm, is classified as stationary without altering the
number. The raw value is what the inverter sent.

Below the two axles is a front/rear comparison: stator temperature delta, front share of absolute
drive torque, HV bus delta, coolant inlet delta, and the electrical power of each axle (volts ×
amps). If either side did not measure, the comparison is left empty rather than filled with a zero.
A torque split is also left empty when both axles read zero torque, because a split of nothing is
undefined, not 50/50. A zero HV current payload (`62 98 61 00 00`) is the inverter no-data default on
a parked car, not a current, so the app leaves the current and any power derived from it empty.

## Charging and power

The charging tab reads the AC inlet on the on-board charger: the plug lock state (unlocked, locked,
or an unknown raw code), the connected pilot cable current rating, and the pack voltage at the
charger terminals. The lock state is the reliable row. The cable rating and the pack voltage scaling
are unverified heuristics, because neither encoding is pinned to a capture, and both are labelled
that way. The app does not read a charge session, a charge curve, or DC fast charging here, and it
stores no charge history. The charge-limit diagnosis that does ship is on [Charging](/docs/charging).

The power tab reads the on-board charger AC input (AC power, AC voltage, AC current, and whether the
charger reports actively charging), the DC-DC converter (HV input, 12 V output voltage and current,
and output power), and three auxiliary-battery signals: 12 V state of charge, 12 V terminal voltage,
and the quiescent current average. The OBC and DC-DC reads go through an extended diagnostic session.
Both were unavailable on the 2026-09-02 software profile, and the app reports them that way instead
of showing zero volts or zero watts. An unavailable converter keeps its row and its source
identifier.

## TCAM: backup battery and GPS

The TCAM screen reads the telematics backup battery. That identifier is discriminated by response
length: a short 4-byte or 7-byte record decodes as battery voltage, state of charge, and, when the
record carries them, internal resistance and temperature. The same identifier returned a 2048-byte
snapshot on the 2026-09-02 vehicle. That layout is not decoded, and the screen shows its length, a
SHA-256 of the full response, and a bounded hex prefix, labelled exploratory. An unknown snapshot
never becomes a battery verdict.

The GPS screen reads the TCAM position fix only when you ask for it, behind a consent gate. It shows
latitude, longitude, altitude, fix type, and the timestamp the module attached. A second read pulls
the multi-constellation GNSS buffer and lists the satellites and the reported fix quality. This is
the car's telematics position, not this computer's location, and it is not a track: the app stores no
route from this screen.

## Steering

The steering screen cannot measure steering angle on this platform. Steering wheel angle is not
served as a diagnostic identifier by any module over DoIP/UDS; it is a periodic broadcast on the Mid
1 CAN bus (frame `0x16`). An earlier build guessed an identifier on the steering-angle module and it
never answered. That guess is gone. The read now reports `not_attempted` with the reason and sends no
request. No angle, rate, or calibration value is fabricated. Zeroing the steering-angle sensor is a
separate service function with its own confirmation; see
[Service functions](/docs/service-functions).

## Service schedule

The service screen reads three interval definitions from the DIM: distance in kilometres, time in
days, and motor-hours. It also reads the counters since the last service and the counters used
toward the next one. The countdown takes the dimension closest to its limit and reports whichever
comes first: distance, time, or motor-hours. The screen prints the arithmetic and the identifier
behind every counter, so a countdown can be checked against the raw values. A counter the DIM does
not answer keeps its failure state instead of reading as zero. The vendor service-dialog status rows
are a separate raw read on the capabilities surface, shown as hex with no decoded value.

## Platforms and honest limits

Module addresses ride the platform registry. A platform that cannot map a module answers
`not_attempted` with a named reason, and no request is sent to a guessed address. That is why a
screen can be empty on one car and populated on another without either being a fault.

CMA is the verified platform, and the observations behind these screens were taken on it. SPA and
SEA carry their own definitions and still need testing on more cars; treat readings from them as
provisional. The steering screen applies to no platform, because the signal is not on the diagnostic bus at all.
