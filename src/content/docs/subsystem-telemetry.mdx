Hanterill has separate views for systems such as climate control, drive units, charging and the 12 V supply. Each view groups the readings relevant to that system. Some views also compare related readings and flag values that do not agree.

## 1. Climate & Heat Pump (`CCM`)

Modern electric Volvos and Polestars use a shared thermal system that heats and cools both the passenger cabin and the high-voltage battery pack. The **Climate** screen reads the **Climate Control Module (CCM)** and **Battery Energy Control Module (BECM)** to show:

- **Heat pump and A/C compressor state**: Compressor speed, power draw, and high-side / low-side refrigerant pressures.
- **Coolant loop temperatures and deltas**: Inlet and outlet temperatures across the cabin heater loop, battery thermal loop, and drivetrain radiator loop, with the temperature difference (*delta*) calculated automatically so you can see whether heat is actually being transferred.
- **High-voltage PTC heater and chiller valves**: Whether the electric auxiliary heater or battery chiller valve is active.
- **Cabin sensors and blend doors**: Interior temperature sensors, evaporator temperature, and the status of the cabin air-distribution dampers.

## 2. Drive Units (`VCU1`, `IHFA`, `IEM`)

On dual-motor cars, the front axle is driven by the **Front High-Voltage Inverter (IHFA)** and the rear axle is driven by the **Rear Axle Drive Inverter (IEM / ERAD)**, coordinated by the **Vehicle Control Unit (VCU1)**.

- **Side-by-side front and rear comparison**: Compares inverter temperature, motor stator temperature, coolant temperature, DC bus voltage, and torque delivery between the front and rear axles.
- **Single-motor awareness**: On single-motor cars, the unpopulated axle is left cleanly blank rather than displayed as fake zeros.
- **Parked rotor drift filter**: Resolver sensors inside electric motors can report tiny sub-RPM numbers from electrical noise even when the car is parked. When the car is stationary, Hanterill labels that noise cleanly at the display layer while preserving the raw sensor reading underneath.

## 3. Power & DC-DC Converter (`OBC` & `BMS`)

Because an electric car has no alternator belts, a **DC-DC converter** steps the 400 V traction battery down to ~14 V to power the car's lights, computers, and 12 V battery. The **Power** screen shows:

- **DC-DC converter output voltage and current** feeding the 12 V system.
- **12 V rail agreement across modules**: Compares the voltage measured at the 12 V battery post against the supply voltage reported by the central gateway, brake controller, and instrument cluster. A large drop on one module points to a corroded ground strap or poor connector.

## 4. Steering (`PSCM` & `SAS`)

Reads the **Steering Angle Sensor (SAS)** and **Power Steering Control Module (PSCM)**:

- **Steering wheel angle (degrees) and turn rate (degrees/second)**.
- **Calibration status**: Confirms whether the steering angle sensor has a valid zero-point calibration.
- **Note on live steering updates**: On some CMA vehicles, the diagnostic gateway only updates the diagnostic steering-angle readout at key-on, while continuous millisecond-by-millisecond steering movement travels on the car's internal CAN bus. Hanterill notes this clearly on screen so a static diagnostic number is never mistaken for a broken sensor.

## 5. Telematics, TCAM Backup Battery & GPS (`TCAM`)

The **Telematics and Connectivity Antenna Module (TCAM)** runs the car's LTE/5G cellular connection, emergency SOS call system, Bluetooth phone-as-key, and satellite positioning (GNSS/GPS).

### TCAM emergency backup battery health (with automatic fallback)
Inside the TCAM sits a small dedicated backup battery so the car can place an emergency SOS call even if the main 12 V battery is disconnected in a collision. When that small backup battery ages, the dashboard often displays an *"eCall service required"* warning.

- Hanterill reads the TCAM backup battery's **State of Health, charge level, voltage, and temperature**.
- **Automatic fallback on newer TCAM software**: On some software versions, the primary backup-battery read returns a 66-byte GPS log record instead of battery health. Hanterill detects that automatically by checking the response layout and switches to the secondary TCAM battery-health read so you still get an accurate backup-battery reading.

### On-request GPS satellite skyplot and signal quality
To protect your privacy, Hanterill **never reads your car's GPS coordinates automatically** during a scan. The **TCAM & GPS** screen only queries satellite positioning when you explicitly click **Read GPS fix**:

- **Live satellite skyplot**: Shows how many GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, and BeiDou satellites the roof antenna currently sees, their elevation and azimuth in the sky, and their signal-to-noise ratio (`C/N0` in dB-Hz) to diagnose a water-damaged shark-fin antenna.
- **Privacy by default**: Even if you click **Read GPS fix** to test the antenna, exact coordinates are excluded from printable inspection reports by default and scrubbed from support bundles.

## 6. Service Schedule (`DIM`)

Reads the **Driver Information Module (DIM)** behind the steering wheel to show the car's internal maintenance counters:

- **Distance remaining (km / miles)** and **time remaining (days / months)** until the next scheduled service.
- **Engine hours / operating time** since the last service reset.
- **Counter plausibility check**: Flags an advisory if one service counter was reset without the others, or if the distance since last service exceeds the car's total odometer mileage.
