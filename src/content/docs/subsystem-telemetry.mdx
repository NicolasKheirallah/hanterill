Hanterill has separate diagnostic screens for climate, drive units, power, steering, telematics, GPS and service schedule. Each screen asks for the readings relevant to that system. The available fields depend on the car and on which control units answer.

## Climate and drive units

**Climate** can show refrigerant pressure and temperature, compressor activity, heater temperatures, battery cooling readings and fan output. A blank field means that value was not returned. The screen identifies the source unit for a reading when it can.

**Drive Units** can show front and rear motor readings such as speed, torque, voltage and temperature. The app may compare the two sides when both answer. A missing rear unit on a car built with one motor is not a failed rear motor.

## Power and steering

**Power** groups available high-voltage and 12 V supply readings. Different units may report related voltages; a difference is something to examine with the car's operating state in mind. It does not alone prove that a cable or ground connection is faulty.

**Steering** shows the steering values and status the car reports. A reading that does not update as expected may reflect how that unit supplies diagnostic data. Check for fault codes and repeat the read before treating it as a failed sensor. Calibration is a separate service action that changes the car.

## Telematics, GPS and service schedule

**Telematics** can show the backup battery and other available module readings. **GPS** reads the car's position only when you request a fix. Review location data before sharing an export. These screens report what the unit returned; they cannot by themselves identify the cause of a weak signal or an emergency-call warning.

**Service Schedule** shows the maintenance counters the car supplies, such as remaining distance, time and operating hours. Some counters can be unavailable. Reading a counter does not reset it; a reset is a separate confirmed service action.

Across these screens, use the reading state and capture time. Do not turn a missing field into zero or treat a single outlying value as a diagnosis.
