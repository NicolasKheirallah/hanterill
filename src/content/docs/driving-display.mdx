The **Driving and display** screen reads the instrument cluster, the module the car itself trusts to show the driver what is happening. It answers the questions a buyer or an owner actually asks: how much has this car really been driven, and when is the next service due?

## The odometer verdict

The headline card is the odometer. The car keeps several copies of the mileage in different modules (the cluster, the gateway, the battery module and others), and the screen reads them together:

- It states the cross-check outright, for example *"6 of 6 copies agree"*.
- If copies disagree, or a tamper flag is set anywhere, the card says so with a warning tone.
- The Vehicle screen adds the wider picture: the mileage consensus across every module that keeps a copy, and the gateway's reset log as a dated odometer history.

## Trip and usage

One read brings the cluster's trip computer: trip distance and drive time, average speed and average energy use, and the range the car is showing. The overview also includes the long-term trip counters and the trip reset counters, so you can see whether the displayed figures were recently zeroed.

## Service intervals

The cluster tracks the service schedule in its own counters. The screen shows the remaining time and distance to the next service and the 12 V battery care interval, and warns when the cluster's two counter records no longer describe the same service event, which is exactly what you want to know before paying for a service that was already done.

## Display data

The board below the overview gives each of the cluster's display groups a card: its decoded values as stat tiles and its history as a timeline list, with a coverage chip such as *"2 of 72 decoded"*. Groups where nothing has been decoded yet share one honest line instead of filler cards. Charging, messages, driver-assistance and system data follow the same pattern on request: each panel reads the first time you open it rather than putting a burst of traffic on the car at page load.

## Learned energy use

The car learns its own consumption as it is driven. The screen shows the learned consumption per band (likely per speed band, in kWh/100 km on the test car) together with how often each band has been driven. The bands are numbered because their exact edges are not established; the shape of the data is the point, not the last unit.

<Callout kind="note" title="Why some figures are marked *likely*">
Values whose scale could not yet be confirmed against the car are labelled *likely* rather than shown as facts. That label is a promise: when a figure is displayed without it, the scale has been checked.
</Callout>
