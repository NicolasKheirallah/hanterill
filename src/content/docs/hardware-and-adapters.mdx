For the usual Hanterill checks, you need an **ENET cable** and a wired Ethernet port on your computer. The cable joins the car's diagnostic socket to your computer. You do not need a wireless scan dongle or an account.

## Choose an ENET cable

Look for a cable described as **OBD-II to Ethernet (ENET)**. It has a wide plug for the car at one end and a network plug for the computer at the other. It is a cable, so there is no software to install for the cable itself.

A cable that works with one model may not wake a newer car. Volvo EX30, Polestar 4 and some Zeekr vehicles need the cable to carry an activation signal that switches on the car's diagnostic Ethernet connection. Some inexpensive ENET cables omit it. If you are buying for one of these cars, check that the cable is described as suitable for that vehicle's Ethernet diagnostic connection. A generic claim that it works with another brand is not enough.

If the car is awake but your computer never shows an Ethernet connection, check the cable and its fit before changing software settings. [Troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting) walks through the checks in order. Do not modify the cable or probe the car's socket unless you know how to work safely with automotive electrical equipment.

## Connect the cable to your computer

If your computer has a network socket, plug the cable in there. Otherwise, use a standard USB-to-Ethernet adapter. A dock with a working network socket is also suitable.

Leave the adapter's network settings on **automatic**. The car and computer establish a direct local connection. Your computer may report that this network has no internet; that is expected and does not mean the cable has failed. You can keep Wi-Fi on for other tasks.

## Why a wireless scan dongle is different

A common Bluetooth or Wi-Fi OBD dongle uses a different connection from the Ethernet link Hanterill uses for its regular vehicle checks. It cannot replace an ENET cable for reading the supported battery, fault and module screens.

## When a separate CAN adapter matters

Some fast signals are broadcast only on the car's internal networks. They may need a separate CAN adapter and access to the relevant internal network. This is optional specialist work. An ENET cable is enough for Hanterill's regular connection, battery, fault-code and service screens. The [communication guide](/docs/architecture) explains the difference in plain language.
