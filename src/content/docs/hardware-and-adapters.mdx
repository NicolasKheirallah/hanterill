For the usual Hanterill checks, you need an **ENET cable** and a wired Ethernet port on your computer. The cable joins the car's diagnostic socket to your computer. You do not need a wireless scan dongle or an account.

## Choose an ENET cable

Look for a cable described as **OBD-II to Ethernet (ENET)**. It has a wide plug for the car at one end and a network plug for the computer at the other. It is a passive cable with no electronics inside, so there is no software to install for the cable itself.

Listings use many names for the same part: "ENET cable", "OBD-II to Ethernet", "OBD to RJ45", and often "BMW ENET cable" (sold for the ISTA and E-Sys workshop tools). They are all wired the same. Buy whichever passive OBD-II-to-RJ45 cable you find — on eBay, Amazon, or at a car-parts shop — and it will work.

The same ENET cable is used for all models Hanterill supports, including Volvo EX30, Polestar 4 and Zeekr. Models differ in what has been verified in software, not in hardware; [Supported vehicles](/docs/supported-vehicles) tracks that per model.

If the car is awake but your computer never shows an Ethernet connection, check both plugs and the computer's Ethernet adapter before changing software settings. [Troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting) walks through the checks in order.

## Connect the cable to your computer

Any wired Ethernet port works: the built-in socket on your computer, a **USB-C hub or dock with an RJ45 port**, or a dedicated USB Ethernet adapter. The [adapter list](#adapters-that-work-and-ones-that-do-not) below names chipsets and makes that are known to work.

Leave the adapter's network settings on **automatic**. The car and computer establish a direct local connection. Your computer may report that this network has no internet; that is expected and does not mean the cable has failed. You can keep Wi-Fi on for other tasks. On Linux, set the connection to **Link-Local** instead of DHCP once — NetworkManager does not fall back to a link-local address by itself.

The **Connection** screen in the app lists the adapters it sees, ranks the wired ones first, and shows a plain-language verdict for each, so an adapter problem usually announces itself there.

## Adapters that work, and ones that do not

The short version: the adapter must hand your computer a real network interface. Wireless OBD dongles and anything that speaks a serial protocol cannot carry the diagnostic traffic, no matter what the listing promises.

## What a CAN adapter will not do

Hanterill's screens all run over the Ethernet link, and the desktop app does not use a CAN adapter — adding one does not unlock anything. The car's internal CAN buses are explained in the [communication guide](/docs/architecture), including the experimental command-line lane that can passively listen to them; that is specialist tooling, not part of the app you download.
