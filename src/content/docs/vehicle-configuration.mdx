The car stores a record of its factory equipment and current configuration. **Vehicle Configuration** reads that information to help you check which features were fitted and whether a setting has changed since the car was built.

You can open this view from **Vehicle** and print the configuration report. Some entries may remain undecoded; the app should show those as unknown rather than guess their meaning.

## 1. Two records inside the gateway

Hanterill reads two complementary configuration records from the car's central gateway:

1. **The active Car Configuration Map (256 option slots)**: A table of 256 numbered feature slots that the car's modules check when they wake up. Each slot holds a setting code that tells the car what hardware or software feature is currently enabled (for example, whether Pilot Assist, Pixel LED headlights, a heat pump, a Harman Kardon sound system, or a towbar is active).
2. **The Factory Build Record**: The original factory specification written when the car was built, including the **anchor VIN**, **exterior paint code and color name**, **interior upholstery and trim code**, **model year and market**, and the list of factory option packages.

## 2. Searching and reading your car's equipment

On the **Vehicle Configuration** tab, you do not have to decipher numbered slots:

- **Live search and category filter**: Type any word (such as `"audio"`, `"headlight"`, `"towbar"`, `"heat pump"`, or `"cruise"`) to filter the 256 configuration slots immediately.
- **Named versus unmapped slots**: Where a slot's meaning has been verified in the Hanterill catalogue, the app shows the human-readable feature name and its current setting. Slots whose exact factory meaning has not yet been confirmed are shown honestly by their slot number and value rather than guessed.

## 3. Spotting retrofits, software unlocks, or replaced modules

Because Hanterill reads both the **Factory Build Record** (how the car left the factory) and the **active Car Configuration Map** (how the car is configured today), comparing the two helps you spot changes made after the car was built:

- **Retrofitted equipment or software unlocks**: For example, a factory towbar added at a dealer, or a software-enabled feature such as Pilot Assist or a performance upgrade enabled after delivery.
- **Anchor VIN cross-check**: Compares the VIN stored inside the factory build record against the VIN reported by the rest of the car's modules to confirm the central gateway belongs to the vehicle.

## 4. Printable Configuration Report

Click **Print Configuration Report** on the **Vehicle** screen to generate a clean, print-ready build sheet containing your car's identity, factory paint and trim codes, and decoded equipment list. Just like in the [Inspection report](/docs/inspection-reports), you can mask the car's 17-character VIN with one click before printing or saving to PDF.
