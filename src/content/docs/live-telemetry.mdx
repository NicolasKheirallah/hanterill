Some problems only show up while the car is doing something: a weak cell sags under load, a pump
stays quiet until it is hot. Live telemetry shows how a signal moves, rather than a single number.

## How sampling works

The live view is a polling loop. Each tick reads every selected channel once, over
`0x22 ReadDataByIdentifier`, then the loop starts again on the next tick. The channel catalogue
holds 51 channels across the battery, drivetrain, thermal, climate and 12 V groups. A run samples
the ones you selected and nothing else.

A tick is one batch: every channel is asked in turn, and the answers arrive together. That is why a
wide selection at a fast interval is harder on the car than a narrow one. See
[Cadence and budgets](#cadence-and-budgets).

<Callout kind="note" title="A separate push mode exists">
UDS `0x2A ReadDataByPeriodicIdentifier` is a different mechanism: the module registers an identifier
and pushes samples itself, at slow (about 5 s), medium (about 1 s) or fast (about 100 ms) rate, with
1–600 samples, a 30 s read deadline, and a mandatory teardown (`0x2A 0x00`) so the bus is never left
flooding. The live view does not use this path; do not mistake the registration feature for the loop
above.
</Callout>

## Presets

Choosing from 51 channels one at a time is slow, so both front ends ship named selections. Those
lists are not the same.

The desktop app has six groups plus a custom selection:

| Group | Channels | The question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Battery & HV Health | 6 | Is the pack behaving under this load? |
| Fast Charge & Thermal | 6 | Is heat being moved away during a fast charge? |
| Powertrain & Inverters | 5 | What are the motors and inverters doing? |
| 12V & Sleep Current | 4 | Is the auxiliary rail holding up? |
| Climate & HVAC | 5 | Is the cabin and pack loop doing what it should? |
| Full Pack Diagnostics | 6 | A broad battery capture with the cross-signals together |

The CLI has four: battery (16 channels), drivetrain (11), thermal (21) and all (all 51). "All" and
"Thermal" exist only on the CLI; the desktop groups are smaller and aimed at one screen of traces.

If a preset names a channel the car does not support, the gap is reported rather than dropped.

## Cadence and budgets

| | CLI | Desktop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rate | `--hz` 1–5, default 1 | 200, 500, 1000 or 2000 ms (0.5–5 Hz), default 500 ms |
| Count | `--samples` 1–10000, default 1 for `live`, 5 for `record` | none: it runs until you stop it |
| Stop condition | the sample count is reached, or the capture budget ends | Stop, or the one-hour operation deadline |
| Accepted interval | not applicable | 20–5000 ms |

The CLI capture budget is samples × 30 s, clamped to 30–300 s. The desktop has no user-set duration
or count: it samples until you stop it, or until the one-hour deadline ends the run.

Only one stream runs at a time: a second start is refused as busy rather than quietly stopping the
first. Pause, resume and stop are available throughout.

Higher rates are not automatically better. The car's gateway serves every module on one connection,
so a fast capture of many channels competes with itself. A 1 Hz capture of six channels will be far
more trustworthy than 5 Hz of forty.

## Recording to a file

The desktop records as it samples, with no separate save step. Each run becomes a `live-recording`
artifact in the session archive, plus a `live-recording-chunk` every 128 samples, under the id
`recording-{operationId}`. Sessions live in `sessions.json` under the OS data directory; the UI also
keeps the last 10 runs in browser local storage, so the drawer survives a reload.

Each stored sample carries `channelKey`, `value`, `error`, `sequence`, `scheduledMs`, `capturedMs`
and `gap`. The manifest carries `schemaVersion` 1, `id`, `sessionId`, the start and stop times,
`channels`, `intervalMs`, `sampleCount`, `chunkCount`, `terminalState` and `error`.

<Callout kind="caution" title="The desktop timer does not measure lateness">
On the desktop, `scheduledMs` equals `capturedMs` and missed intervals are recorded as 0: a run
that fell behind looks exactly like one that did not. Only the CLI scheduler emits gap markers for
missed intervals, so if you need evidence that a tick was late, record from the CLI.
</Callout>

The CLI writes a file only when you pass `--output`. JSON is `schema_version` 1, kind
`live-recording`, with the start and stop times, preset, `interval_ms`, `sample_iterations`,
channels, coverage and frames. CSV is long form:
`sequence,scheduled_ms,captured_ms,channel,ecu_hex,did_hex,state,value,unit,error`.

Neither the desktop chunks nor the manifest store a decoder version or a unit. The recording is the
samples, not the decode that produced them.

## Replay

Desktop replay runs client-side over stored samples: the recordings drawer, the archive chunks, and
files you import (`.json` or `.csv`). Controls are a range scrubber, play/pause, and a speed button
cycling 1x → 2x → 5x. Every channel in the recording is plotted; the only per-channel control is the
legend toggle.

The CLI `replay` is a different engine: it advances virtual time in one-second steps over a saved
recording and prints the samples due in each window. It is deterministic and never reads a car.

Replay is clearly labelled as replay. A recording never presents itself as a live reading, and a
simulated session never presents itself as a recording.

The charts share one behaviour: a single y-axis per unit, a crosshair tooltip, inside zoom plus a
slider once a channel has more than 40 points, and a scrolling legend. There are no point markers.

## Exporting a trace

Live export and recording export are different shapes, and the headers are the import contract.

- Live JSON is kind `live-telemetry`: `intervalMs`, `channels`, and samples with `sampleIndex`,
  `offsetMs`, `channelKey`, `label`, `value` and `unit`.
- Live wide CSV is `Sample, t_ms (nominal), <label> (<unit>) ...`. One row per tick, one column per
  channel. The timebase is **nominal, not measured**: `t_ms` is the scheduled offset, not an arrival
  clock.
- Recording CSV is long form `Sample, Offset (ms), Channel, Value, Error`, the same shape the
  importer accepts.
- Recording JSON is kind `live-recording`.

CSV formula injection is sanitised, so a channel label starting with `=` cannot run when the file
is opened. The CLI `export <session.json>` writes a bundle, including `live_telemetry.csv` and
`manifest.csv`, so a session can be unpacked without the desktop app.

## Stream health and staleness

There is no host-side system telemetry screen. Nothing charts this computer's CPU, memory or transport timing:

- A per-channel stats table shows current, min, max, mean, sample count, trend, and the age of the
  last sample.
- Per-stream ingest counters track received, flushes, coalesced, actual Hz, gaps and errors.
- A silence watchdog marks a channel as stalled once it stops answering for long enough, instead of
  reading as if it were merely slow.
- A retained chart window keeps 600 samples per channel, so a long run shows recent history rather
  than growing without bound.

Derived metrics are computed from the live channels: pack power (volts × amps), thermal delta across
the pack, combined powertrain current, rear mechanical power, and 12 V power. The car did not send
them.

Vehicle subsystem screens cover the slower questions; live telemetry is for the ones that only exist
while something is moving.

## When to use it

- For battery health, watch pack current and voltage together under acceleration, then compare the
  cell spread at rest against the spread under load.
- For thermal problems, watch coolant in and out, and see whether the delta grows under sustained
  load or stays flat.
- For intermittent faults, record while the fault is happening, then replay it next to the freeze
  frame the module stored.
- Before and after a repair, record the same channels under the same conditions, then compare.
