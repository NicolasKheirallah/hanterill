Hanterill has a lot of screens. This page is the map: what each one is for, and which ones you will
actually use on a normal day.

The short version: **Overview** tells you what the car is, **Fault Codes (DTCs)** and
**Battery Health** are where the real work happens, and **Sessions & Reports** is where everything
you did is kept.

## Vehicle

- **Overview**: the landing page. Car identity, connection state, and a first summary of the
  battery and any faults.
- **Vehicle Entity**: the car's fingerprint. VIN, mileage from several modules, key count and
  uptime, with the odometer cross-check.
- **Connection**: pick an adapter, discover a car, see the link state and its details.
- **Inspection Report**: the scored condition summary. See [Inspection reports](/docs/inspection-reports).
- **Service Schedule**: service intervals and how much distance or time is left.
- **Build Configuration**: the car's factory option list, read from the CEM.
- **GPS Position**: a position fix from the TCAM module, requested only when you press the button.

## Energy and battery

- **Battery Health**: SoH, SoC, pack voltage and current, temperatures, and the cross-signal
  checks. See [Battery diagnostics](/docs/battery-diagnostics).
- **Cell & Thermal Map**: the cell-group data laid out physically over the pack, so a weak spot has
  a location rather than just a number.
- **12V Sleep Drain**: standby current history. Useful when a car has a flat battery in the morning
  and nothing else looks wrong.
- **Charging** and **Power (OBC/DC-DC)**: charging socket state, the 12 V rail and the converters.
- **TCAM Battery**: the emergency backup battery in the telematics module.

## Powertrain and thermal

- **Drive Units**: front and rear motor and inverter telemetry, side by side.
- **ICE engine**: the combustion and hybrid reference lane. On a battery-electric car this screen
  stays quiet by design; it exists for the hybrid variants of the platform.
- **Steering**: steering angle, rate and calibration state. Read-only.
- **HVAC & Thermal**: the refrigerant loop, heater circuit and compressor.

The energy domains (climate, drive units, charging and power) are tabs of one workspace, so a deep
link can land you directly on the panel you need.

## Diagnostics and ECUs

- **Fault Codes (DTCs)**: the scan. Read, triage, clear and export. See [DTC scanning](/docs/dtc-scanning).
- **ECU Inventory**: every module in the registry with its address, what it answered, and what it
  reported. See [ECU reference](/docs/ecu-reference).
- **DID Scanner**: a bounded sweep over identifier ranges across all modules. This is a research
  tool: it finds what a module answers that the catalogue does not know about yet.
- **DTC History Diff**: compare two scans and see what changed.
- **Firmware SBOM**: the software inventory. Every module's part numbers and versions in one list,
  diffable between visits.

## Telemetry and tools

- **Live Telemetry**: selected channels sampled on a fixed cadence and charted, with recording and
  playback.
- **DID Explorer**: read one identifier at a time, with the raw bytes and the decoded value side by
  side. The manual override for when a screen does not show what you need.
- **Service & Routines**: the write operations, each behind an explicit confirmation. See [Safety](/docs/safety).

## System

- **Capabilities**: what this specific car answered it can do, and what this build of the app can
  do. When a screen seems empty, this is the honest explanation.
- **Sessions & Reports**: everything you have captured, with exports and comparisons.
- **Activity & Logs**: the operator trail of what was run, with VIN redaction applied.
- **Settings**: appearance, language, connection policy, privacy and storage.

## Reading a screen

Most values in Hanterill carry three levels of detail, and you can stop at whichever one answers
your question:

1. **Label, value and unit.** The number you came for.
2. **Interpretation.** What it means, how fresh it is, and whether it is measured or derived.
3. **Evidence.** Which module and identifier it came from, the raw bytes, and the decoder version.

That third level is what makes the tool auditable. If a number looks wrong, you can see where it
came from without trusting anyone's summary.

## Missing values are shown as missing

A reading that did not answer is displayed as a gap with a reason, never as a zero. A module that
was skipped is listed as skipped. A scan reports how many modules replied and how many did not.
On a diagnostic tool, a fabricated zero is worse than a visible gap.

## Keyboard

- `Ctrl+K` or `Cmd+K` opens the command palette: jump to any screen, or search for an ECU by name.
- `?` shows the full shortcut list.
- Two-key chords jump straight to common screens, pressing `G` then `O` for overview, `G` then `B`
  for battery, and so on.

## Making it yours
