## Health, charge level and capacity

The battery controller, called the BECM, reports several numbers that sound similar but answer different questions.

| Reading | What it means | How Hanterill gets it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **State of Health (SoH)** | The car's estimate of how much capacity the battery retains compared with when it was new. | Reads the percentage reported by the BECM. |
| **State of Charge (SoC)** | How full the battery is right now. It changes as you drive or charge. | Reads the available charge values reported by the car. |
| **Learned capacity** | How much charge the BECM has learned that individual cell groups can hold. The app shows an average in amp-hours (Ah) when available. | Reads the BECM's cell-group values and calculates their average. |

A car can report **90% SoH and 20% SoC** at the same time. In that example, the controller estimates that the battery can hold about 90% of the charge it held when new, while it is currently charged to 20%. The percentages describe different things.

### How to interpret SoH

Hanterill displays the SoH reported by the BECM. It does not work it out from the range shown on the dashboard. SoH is the controller's estimate, not a laboratory test or a promise that the car will drive a particular distance. Speed, weather, heating, driving style and battery temperature all affect range.

A single SoH reading is useful context. Saved readings from the same car are more useful for seeing whether its estimate changes over time. A small difference between two visits is less informative than a repeated pattern. If the controller does not return a usable SoH value, the app shows that the value is unavailable.

### What usable capacity means

Usable capacity is the energy the car makes available for normal driving, usually expressed in kilowatt-hours (kWh). It is different from the battery's total physical capacity because the car keeps some charge in reserve. It is also different from the energy left in the battery **right now**, which depends on SoC.

The app's **learned capacity** reading is related to usable capacity, but it is not the same number. The BECM supplies learned capacity in amp-hours (Ah). Hanterill compares the average with the original charge capacity expected for the detected battery variant and shows the difference as **capacity fade**. This percentage is calculated by the app from a BECM reading and the battery variant's reference value.

Amp-hours measure charge; kilowatt-hours measure energy. The kWh capacity bar on this website uses sample figures to explain the idea. It is not a live kWh measurement from your car. In the app, use the reported SoH and learned capacity as the available evidence. Pay attention to any provisional label: it means the interpretation still needs more verification for that vehicle.

## Why charge percentages can differ

The driver display and the battery controller may use different scales for charge level. The car can also keep a reserve at the top and bottom of the battery's operating range. Hanterill places the available charge readings side by side. A small difference is not, on its own, proof of a fault.

## Cell groups and the Cell Map

A battery contains many cell groups. The Cell Map shows the voltage of each group that answered during the scan. It helps you find a group that stands out even when the overall battery reading looks ordinary. The map uses the layout detected for the vehicle; the number of groups varies by battery.

Hanterill shows the lowest, highest and average cell-group voltage. **Voltage spread** is the gap between the highest and lowest reading, measured in millivolts (mV), or thousandths of a volt. It describes the pack at the moment of the scan. Charge level, temperature and recent driving or charging can affect it. Compare scans made under similar conditions; a group that repeatedly stands out is more meaningful than one unusual result.

Some cell groups may not answer. The app leaves their positions blank and marks the scan as incomplete. You can use **Re-read missing modules** to try again. A missing reading is never treated as zero. Where the BECM supplies separate cell-group health or learned-capacity readings, the app can summarize those as well; they are different from the voltage spread shown on the Cell Map.

## Temperature, power and other readings

Battery Health shows the temperatures and coolant readings the car provides. A difference between warm and cool sensors can be useful context, but it does not by itself identify a faulty part. The page also shows available charge and discharge power limits. These are the limits the car reports at the time of the scan, not a guarantee of the power you will always get.

**Thermal exposure** is a separate, optional read. Where supported, Hanterill asks another car control unit for its record of time spent in different temperature ranges. The displayed history is cumulative; it does not describe only the current trip. If the car does not return it, the app says so. Hanterill also shows the available 12 V supply reading, which belongs to the car's low-voltage system.

## Checks and missing data

Hanterill compares readings that should agree, such as the battery's reported voltage and the sum of the cell-group voltages. It can also check reported power against voltage and current, and whether current looks plausible while the car is parked. A disagreement may come from an incomplete scan, a changing operating state or a reading that needs closer inspection. It is a prompt to investigate, not a diagnosis from one number.

The page distinguishes a value that was measured, one calculated from readings, one marked provisional and one that was not reported. Missing or uncertain data should not be interpreted as a healthy or unhealthy result.

## Compare readings over time

Save a session with a battery report to add a point to **Battery Health Trend**. The trend shows SoH, SoC and, when available, the **spread of cell-group SoH values**. That last figure compares the health percentages reported for cell groups; it is **not** the voltage spread from the Cell Map. The trend groups visits by vehicle and leaves uncaptured values blank. You can export its table as a CSV file.

For a closer look at cell voltages, **Compare cell captures** compares two cell-map captures that you supply. It compares the same cell positions in the two captures. The saved-session trend does not automatically compare complete cell maps.
