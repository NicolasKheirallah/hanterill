## 1. State of Health (SoH) and usable capacity

**State of Health (SoH)** is the battery management computer's own measure of how much usable charge capacity your high-voltage battery has today compared with when it left the factory, expressed as a percentage.

- **Read directly, never guessed**: Hanterill does not invent or estimate your State of Health from range guesses. It reads the exact health percentage reported by the car's battery management computer, alongside the estimated usable energy in kilowatt-hours (kWh) compared with the pack's nominal new capacity.
- **How lithium-ion packs age normally**: Most electric vehicle batteries lose a few percent of capacity during their first year or two as the protective chemical layer inside the cells stabilizes, and then age much more slowly over the following years. Comparing your reading across multiple saved sessions is the best way to see that steady curve.

## 2. State of Charge (SoC) and cross-checking across modules

**State of Charge (SoC)** tells you how full the battery is right now. On the **Battery Health** and **Cross-Check** screens, Hanterill compares the charge percentage reported by the battery management computer against the charge figures reported by the central gateway and driver display:

- Why the numbers can differ slightly: The dashboard shows a **usable (display) State of Charge** that reserves a small safety buffer at the very top and bottom of the pack to protect the battery from overcharging or deep discharge. The battery module itself also tracks the raw technical charge level. Showing them side by side explains why "100% on the dash" is slightly below the physical ceiling of the cells.

## 3. Cell-group voltages and the Cell Map

A high-voltage EV battery is built from dozens of **series cell groups** wired end to end. On the reference Polestar 2 and CMA electric Volvos (78 kWh and 82 kWh packs), there are **27 modules**, each holding **4 series cell groups**, for **108 cell-group voltages** in total.

For every cell group in the pack, Hanterill calculates and displays:

- **Minimum and Maximum cell-group voltage**: the lowest and highest group in the pack at the moment of the scan.
- **Pack average (mean)**: the average voltage across all populated cell groups.
- **Cell spread (Delta, in millivolts)**: the gap between the highest and lowest cell group (`Max - Min`), expressed in thousandths of a volt (`mV`).

### What a healthy cell spread looks like

When the car is parked and the battery has rested, all cell groups should sit very close to the same voltage:

- **Under 10 mV**: Well balanced. Typical of a healthy pack at rest.
- **10 to 25 mV**: Slightly widened spread, which can happen right after fast charging or spirited driving before the balancing circuit has finished equalizing the cells.
- **Over 30 mV at rest**: Worth watching closely. If one specific cell group stays noticeably lower than its neighbors both at high charge and low charge, it can limit the usable range of the entire pack.

### Automatic support for 11 battery pack layouts

Hanterill adapts the **Cell Map** automatically to your car's exact battery layout (108-group and 96-group CMA EV packs, 96-group and 102-group center-tunnel plug-in hybrid packs, and 107-group, 108-group, and 110-group SEA packs). You can also inspect the pack in an interactive **3D physical layout** that shows where each module sits in the floor, footwell, or center tunnel.

### Retrying missed battery modules

Some battery modules may not answer during a scan. Hanterill leaves their readings blank and offers **Retry missed modules**. It does not turn a missing value into zero or fill it from another module.

## 4. Pack temperatures and the 8-band lifetime temperature history

Temperature has a major influence on both daily driving range and long-term battery aging. Hanterill reads two sets of thermal information from the battery computer:

1. **Current pack and coolant temperatures**: The lowest, highest, and average temperature sensors inside the pack right now, plus the inlet and outlet coolant temperatures and the status of the high-voltage coolant pump and chiller valve.
2. **Lifetime temperature residence history (8 temperature bands)**: The battery computer keeps a cumulative log of how much of its life the battery has spent in **eight temperature bands**:
   - Below `-10 °C`
   - `-10 °C` to `0 °C`
   - `0 °C` to `10 °C`
   - `10 °C` to `20 °C`
   - `20 °C` to `30 °C`
   - `30 °C` to `40 °C`
   - `40 °C` to `50 °C`
   - Above `50 °C`

When inspecting a used EV, this 8-band histogram lets you see immediately whether the battery has lived a normal life in moderate temperatures (`10 °C` to `30 °C`) or spent unusual time above `40 °C`.

## 5. Multi-session Battery Health History and Cell Comparison

Every time you scan your car, Hanterill links the battery reading to your vehicle's local history:

- **Battery Health Trend chart**: Tracks **State of Health (SoH)**, **State of Charge (SoC)**, and **Cell Spread (mV)** across every saved visit for that car, and lets you export the trend table to **CSV** with one click.
- **Cell-by-cell comparison between sessions**: Pick any two saved sessions in **Sessions & Reports -> Cell History Diff** to compare all 108 cell groups by physical position, making it easy to verify whether a weak cell group improved after balancing or continued to drift.

## 6. Built-in consistency cross-checks

Whenever you read the battery, Hanterill automatically checks the car's numbers against each other:

- **Pack voltage versus sum of cells**: Adds up all individual cell-group voltages and compares that total against the overall pack voltage sensor. They should agree within a couple of volts.
- **Parked current plausibility**: Checks that the pack current sensor reads near zero when the car is parked with charging and climate turned off.
- **Thermal spread**: Checks the gap between the warmest and coolest temperature probes inside the pack.
