The high-voltage battery has its own computer, the BECM (Battery Energy Control Module). It
already tracks everything about the pack: how much charge is left, how healthy it is, the voltage
of every cell group, and how warm it is. Hanterill asks that computer questions rather than
estimating from other signals.

<SafetyNotice level="read-only">
Everything on this page is a read. Hanterill asks the BECM for values and decodes the answers. It
does not write to the module and does not change how the pack is managed.
</SafetyNotice>

## State of Health, the short version

State of Health (SoH) is the number you want before buying a used EV. It compares the pack's
current capacity to what it had when new. A pack at 91% SoH has lost 9% of its original capacity.

The BECM calculates this itself. Hanterill reads it through the identifier `0x496D`, where the
answer is a 32-bit integer in hundredths of a percent.

  service="ReadDataByIdentifier, SoH on the BECM"

  note="The payload 0x0000239A is 9114. Divided by 100 that is 91.14% SoH. This exact decode is verified against the reference car."

## What else you get

- State of Charge (SoC): how full the pack is, read as a live channel (`0x4A89`)
- Pack voltage (`0x4803`) and pack current (`0x4802`), read live
- Every cell-group potential on the pack layout in force: 108 on CMA (27 modules of 4 groups)
- Minimum, maximum and spread across those groups
- Pack temperature channels (`0x4980`), shown as provisional: the sensor-to-module mapping is not
  established yet, so the values are displayed as pack channels, never as one module's temperature
- Per-cell SoH and learned-capacity blocks (`0x480E`, `0x4982`, `0xDA02`)
- Cross-checks that compare one measured signal against another
- BECM hardware and software part numbers, read with the standard identity identifiers

## Why a cell matrix instead of one number

One average voltage hides problems. A pack where every cell group sits within a few millivolts of
its neighbours is balanced. A pack where one group is consistently lower than the rest has a weak
spot forming.

That is why Hanterill reads the cell groups as a block rather than trusting a single summary value.
The website's [battery section](/features/battery-health) shows the same matrix with sample data.

<Callout kind="note" title="How the cell groups are read">
Per-module blocks live at `0x4B10` through `0x4B2A`: 27 identifiers, one per module, four cell
groups each. The layout comes from a catalogue entry for the car's pack, not a number written into
the code. A 102-cell hybrid pack is treated as complete at 102.
</Callout>

## The temperature history

The BECM also stores a lifetime record: how many hours the pack has spent in each temperature band.
Hanterill shows it as a histogram with the total operating hours and how much of that time was in
the comfortable window. Heat is what ages a battery, so time spent very hot or very cold is worth
seeing before you buy.

## Cross-checks

Hanterill compares signals that should agree with each other and reports where they do not: the
reported pack voltage against the sum of the cell groups, calculated power against reported power,
current flow while parked, and more. There are ten of these checks.

The verdict vocabulary matters more than the count. A check can come back consistent, a warning, a
signal disagreement, decoder uncertain, or insufficient data. An input that never answered is
recorded as missing, never treated as a pass. No check claims a hardware fault, and none goes beyond
a warning.

## Reading it over the wire

After routing activation, Hanterill opens an extended session with the BECM and reads each
identifier:

<DidReference
  rows={[
    ["0x496D", "Battery state of health"],
    ["0x4803", "Pack voltage"],
    ["0x4802", "Pack current"],
    ["0x4B10-0x4B2A", "Cell-group potentials, one block per module"],
    ["0xF190", "VIN, for session correlation"],
  ]}
/>

Live views use the periodic identifier read (`0x2A`) where the module supports it: one registration
instead of asking the same question in a loop.

## Other platforms

<Callout kind="warning" title="Not hardware-verified on SPA and SEA">
SPA and SEA1 carry their own BECM definitions: different cell counts and different payload
contracts. The same identifier number can be a 4-byte value on CMA and a 2-byte one on SPA, so they
are separate definitions, never shared ones. They ship under community-research provenance and
nothing is verified against a live SPA or SEA car yet. Battery data from those cars is marked
unverified and should not be used for decisions.
</Callout>
