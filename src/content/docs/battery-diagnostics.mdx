The high-voltage battery has its own computer, the BECM (Battery Energy Control Module). It
already tracks everything about the pack: how much charge is left, how healthy it is, the voltage
of every cell group, and how warm it is. Hanterill asks that computer questions rather than
estimating from other signals.

<SafetyNotice level="read-only">
Everything on this page is a read. Hanterill asks the BECM for values and decodes the answers. It
does not write to the module and does not change how the pack is managed.
</SafetyNotice>

## Three things called "battery health"

The phrase covers three different numbers in this app, and only one of them is handed over by the
car. You have to keep them apart to read the battery screens correctly.

| What people call it | Where it comes from | What it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The **State of Health** percentage | Read from the BECM. The car calculates it. | How much capacity is left compared with new. |
| The **per-cell summary** | Calculated by Hanterill from three per-cell blocks. | How uniform the cells are, and what their average condition is. |
| The **report health score** (out of 100) | Calculated by Hanterill's scoring rules. | How the whole car looks, with the battery as one input. |

## State of Health: read, not calculated

State of Health (SoH) compares the pack's current capacity to what it had when new. A pack at 91%
SoH has lost 9% of its original capacity. It is the number you want before buying a used EV.

The BECM calculates it. Hanterill reads the answer and validates it:

- On the CMA platform the answer is a four-byte integer in hundredths of a percent. A raw value of
  9114 is displayed as **91.14%**.
- A second platform sends the same kind of figure under a different contract: two bytes with the
  same hundredths-of-a-percent scaling, but a separate definition. It is community research and is
  not verified against a car.
- Every SoH reading is range-checked to 0–100%. A payload of the wrong length, or a value
  outside that range, never becomes a number.

SoH is a report from the battery controller, not a capacity test performed by Hanterill. The
battery certificate says so in as many words, and prints no letter grade.

## State of Charge: read live

State of Charge (SoC) is how full the pack is right now. It is read live and shown at the time of
capture. SoH and SoC answer different questions: a pack can be 90% healthy and 20% charged at the
same time.

The BECM reports two independent SoC figures. Hanterill shows both and compares them:

- If the two agree, nothing is raised.
- If they differ by **2 percentage points or more**, the app raises a review item and shows both
  readings. It never averages them and never silently picks one.

A separate per-cell SoC block adds a mean and a spread across the cells. The 12 V support battery
has its own state-of-charge assessment, from its rested voltage; that one feeds the report score and
is described in [Inspection reports](/docs/inspection-reports).

## What Hanterill calculates: the per-cell summaries

Hanterill reads and summarises three per-cell blocks:

| Block | Unit | What is reported |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per-cell SoH | % | mean and spread |
| Learned capacity | Ah | mean |
| Per-cell SoC | % | mean and spread |

Each block is a series of 16-bit slots, with each bit worth 0.01 of the unit. A full CMA pack has
108 slots, but the decode is topology-aware and also accepts 96, 102, 107, 110 and 120. A hybrid
pack is therefore not rejected for not being 108.

From each block the app works out:

- **mean** = the sum of the raw values, divided by the number of slots, divided by 100
- **spread** = the highest value minus the lowest

Each block is range-gated: per-cell SoH 0–100%, learned capacity 0–400 Ah, per-cell SoC 0–100%. If
a block's mean falls outside its range, both the mean and the spread are left empty rather than
defaulted. If no block answers at all, the whole summary is empty. A value the car did not provide
is shown as missing, never as zero.

## Why a cell matrix instead of one number

One average voltage hides problems. A pack where every cell group sits within a few millivolts of
its neighbours is balanced. A pack where one group is consistently lower than the rest has a weak
spot forming.

That is why Hanterill reads the cell groups as a block rather than trusting a single summary value.
The website's [battery section](/features/battery-health) shows the same matrix with sample data.

<Callout kind="note" title="How the cell groups are read">
Per-module blocks live at `0x4B10` through `0x4B2A`: 27 identifiers, one per module, four cell
groups each. The layout comes from a catalogue entry for the car's pack, not a number written into
the code. A 102-cell hybrid pack is treated as complete at 102.
</Callout>

### What the balance bands mean

"Balance" is the spread between the highest and the lowest cell-group potential at rest. Hanterill
bands that spread for a human reader:

| Spread at rest | Band | How to read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 mV or less | Tight | Every group is sitting close to its neighbours. |
| 20–35 mV | Normal | Ordinary rest-voltage tolerance for this chemistry. |
| 35–60 mV | Watch | One group may be lagging, or balancing may be weak. Worth a repeat reading. |
| Above 60 mV | Imbalanced | Persistent spread points at a weak cell or a balancing fault. |

Two rules come with those bands:

- They apply only to a complete cell read. A partial sweep is a coverage gap and is shown as
  "not banded"; it never earns a verdict from an incomplete set of cells.
- They are review bands, not vehicle specifications and not score points. They tell you where to
  look. Hanterill does not deduct score for them.

## The temperature history

The BECM also stores a lifetime record: how many hours the pack has spent in each of twelve
ten-degree temperature bands, from +75 °C down to −35 °C. Hanterill shows it as a histogram with the
total operating hours and how much of that time was in the comfortable window.

- The **optimal-window percentage** is the share of lifetime hours spent in the three middle bands,
  centred on 35 °C, 25 °C and 15 °C, the 15–35 °C window the decoder names.
- The **thermal stress index** (0–100) weights the hot bands (10, 6, 4, 2) and the cold bands
  (2, 5, 10), scales by lifetime hours, and caps at 100. A low number means little time was spent
  where the pack ages fastest.

Heat is what ages a battery, so time spent very hot or very cold is worth seeing before you buy.
Live pack temperatures are a separate matter: the sensor-to-module mapping is not established yet,
so those values are displayed as pack channels, marked provisional, and never as one module's
temperature.

## Cross-checks

Hanterill compares signals that should agree with each other and reports where they do not:
reported pack voltage against the sum of the cell groups, calculated power against reported power,
current flow while parked, and more. There are fourteen of these checks, and four of them are
battery-specific:

- per-cell SoH spread across the pack
- per-cell SoH against the pack-level SoH
- per-cell SoC spread
- per-cell capacity plausibility

The verdict vocabulary matters more than the count. A check can come back consistent, a warning, a
signal disagreement, decoder uncertain, or insufficient data. An input that never answered is
recorded as missing, never treated as a pass. No check claims a hardware fault, and none goes beyond
a warning.

## What the battery contributes to the report score

The scored inspection report takes only part of the battery picture:

- SoH drives the score. Below 70% deducts 30 points, 70–80% deducts 14, 80–90% deducts 4, and
  90% or above deducts nothing. A pack below 70% is below the published warranty floor.
- The 12 V rested rail also scores: below 12.2 V deducts 16, and 12.2–12.5 V deducts 6.
- Cell balance and thermal spread do not score. They stay observations and review bands.
- If no SoH reading arrives, the score is left empty and the verdict is inconclusive. Hanterill
  does not guess a score from partial evidence, and it does not treat a missing reading as a zero.

The full arithmetic, the verdict bands and the two hard overrides are in
[Inspection reports](/docs/inspection-reports).

## Reading it over the wire

After routing activation, Hanterill opens an extended session with the BECM and reads each
identifier:

    ["0x4B10-0x4B2A", "Cell-group potentials, one block per module"],
    ["0xF190", "VIN, for session correlation"],

/>

Live views use the periodic identifier read (`0x2A`) where the module supports it: one registration
instead of asking the same question in a loop.

## Other platforms

<Callout kind="warning" title="Not hardware-verified on SPA and SEA">
SPA and SEA1 carry their own BECM definitions: different cell counts and different payload
contracts. The same identifier number can be a 4-byte value on CMA and a 2-byte one on SPA, so they
are separate definitions, never shared ones. They ship under community-research provenance and
nothing is verified against a live SPA or SEA car yet. Battery data from those cars is marked
unverified and should not be used for decisions.
</Callout>
