## Pairs of terms that are easy to mix up

A few terms sound similar but measure very different things:

- **SoH versus SoC**: **State of Health (SoH)** is how much usable storage capacity the battery pack still holds compared with when it left the factory. **State of Charge (SoC)** is how full the battery is right now, like a fuel gauge. A battery pack can be at 93% State of Health while sitting at 20% State of Charge.
- **Battery SoH, cell delta, and the Inspection Report score**: Three separate numbers. SoH is reported by the battery management module as an overall capacity percentage. Cell delta is the voltage gap (in millivolts) between the highest and lowest individual cells at that moment. The Inspection Report score is an overall 0-to-100 vehicle evaluation that combines battery health, cell balance, fault codes, and module responses. See [Battery diagnostics](/docs/battery-diagnostics).
- **ECU versus DID**: An **ECU (Electronic Control Unit)** is a physical computer box inside the car (such as the brake controller or climate controller). A **DID (Data Identifier)** is a single numbered measurement or setting inside that computer (such as cabin temperature or software version). One ECU answers hundreds of different DIDs.
- **DTC versus freeze frame**: A **DTC (Diagnostic Trouble Code)** is the fault code itself, telling you what went wrong. A **freeze frame** is the snapshot of vehicle conditions (speed, voltage, temperature, mileage) recorded at the exact moment that fault occurred.
- **Compatible versus supported**: **Compatible** means Hanterill can connect to the vehicle and read core identification and fault codes. **Supported** means the full set of subsystem readings and formulas has been verified on live cars of that platform. See [Supported vehicles](/docs/supported-vehicles).
- **Provisional versus verified**: A reading marked **verified** has had its unit, scaling formula, and physical range confirmed on live vehicles. A reading marked **provisional** is decoded using a working formula that is still being cross-checked across additional models and trim levels.

## Common diagnostic actions explained

When Hanterill communicates with your car over the diagnostic cable, almost everything it does comes
down to six standard types of messages:

<SpecList rows={[
  ["Open a diagnostic session", "Tells a module to wake up its diagnostic interface so it can answer detailed identification and sensor reads."],
  ["Keep-alive heartbeat (Tester Present)", "A quiet periodic signal sent while a scan is running so the car does not put its modules back to sleep mid-read."],
  ["Read a data value", "Asks a module for one specific measurement or identity record (such as battery voltage, coolant temperature, or VIN)."],
  ["Read fault codes", "Asks a module to list any stored, pending, or permanent Diagnostic Trouble Codes and their freeze-frame snapshots."],
  ["Clear stored fault codes", "Instructs a module to erase its stored fault history. Because this changes the car's state, Hanterill always asks for your confirmation first."],
  ["Run a service routine", "Starts a built-in maintenance procedure inside a module (such as retracting the parking brake for pad replacement or resetting the service reminder) after you confirm the safety prompt."],
]} />
