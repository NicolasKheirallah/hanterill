Hanterill separates reading from changing at the build level, not with a settings switch. What the
running binary can do is reported by a capability manifest, and the interface renders only what the
binary can execute.

## Read-only by construction

A default build of any Hanterill artifact (desktop app, CLI, library) transmits only read-only UDS
traffic: session control (`0x10`), read DTC information (`0x19`), read data by identifier (`0x22`),
scaling reads (`0x24`) and tester present (`0x3E`), plus two exact carve-outs:

- `0x31 0x03` request routine results, which reports the stored outcome of a routine that already
  ran and never starts or stops anything;
- `0x2A` periodic read send-once and stop, for one on-demand sample and its teardown.

The transport layer rejects every other service and subfunction at runtime. There is no build in
which Hanterill flashes modules, unlocks security access (`0x27`) or writes by identifier (`0x2E`).

## Operations that change the vehicle

- Clearing diagnostic information (`0x14`)
- Starting routines (`0x31`), including EPB service mode and calibration helpers
- Adaptations and writes by identifier
- ECU reset (`0x11`), used only by the opt-in force-reset loop in the clear path

<SafetyNotice level="adaptation" title="Not in released builds">
These are compiled only with the non-default `unsafe-write-ops` feature. A released binary answers
them with a typed refusal; there is no hidden way to turn a standard build into a write-capable
one. The routine identifiers are reverse-engineered, and even write-capable builds keep steering
angle and ADAS targets out of reach: those must not be actuated without the manufacturer's
procedure. The clear path additionally captures freeze frames before erasing (the erase wipes
them), re-reads to verify and reports the stubborn set. See the repository's `docs/safety.md` for
the full flow.
</SafetyNotice>

## Traffic discipline

Diagnostic queries use bounded loops, request budgets and cooperative cancellation, so a scan can
not flood the gateway. Sessions run with the vehicle parked, in normal usage mode; the DTC clear
loop refuses the force-reset step while the car reports a driving mode.

## High voltage

<SafetyNotice level="high-voltage" title="High-voltage systems are hazardous">
Electric vehicles carry hazardous high-voltage systems. Reading battery data over a diagnostic link
does not de-energise anything and does not make physical high-voltage service safe. HV service
requires the manufacturer's procedures, equipment and training.
</SafetyNotice>

## Not a replacement for manufacturer tooling

Hanterill is independent and is not affiliated with Volvo Cars, Polestar or Geely. Do not make a
safety-critical repair decision on Hanterill output alone, especially on Testing or Research
platforms where decoding is not verified on a live vehicle.

## If something looks wrong

Stop. Do not run routines to "fix" a reading you do not understand. Export the session, compare it
against a known-good reference or a second scan, and ask in the project's discussions before
acting. The [support bundle](/docs/privacy) export is scrubbed for sharing.
