Charging is read from the on-board charger (OBC). The app asks the OBC about the inlet it controls:
is the plug locked, what current can the connected cable carry, and what voltage sits at the charger
terminals. On top of those three readings sits a diagnosis that tries to explain why charge power is
low, and declines to explain when the evidence does not establish a cause.

<SafetyNotice level="read-only">
The charging screen is a read. It does not start, stop, schedule, or limit a charge, and it does not
change any charge setting in the car. Every value is decoded from an `0x22 ReadDataByIdentifier`
answer.
</SafetyNotice>

## What the charging screen reads

Three signals come from the OBC:

- the charge socket lock solenoid state, unlocked, locked, or an unknown raw code
- the connected pilot cable current rating in amperes
- the traction pack voltage at the charger terminals in volts

Each row carries its own state: measured, unsupported, failed, or not attempted. A failed row keeps
its source identifier and shows no value, so an empty row still tells you what was asked.

## AC inlet, not a charge session

The screen reads the AC inlet on the on-board charger. There is no DC fast-charge screen, and the app
does not read a charging session as an object: it cannot tell you when a session started, how long it
ran, how much energy went in, or what the charge curve looked like. No charge history is stored.

That limit is worth stating plainly because it shapes what the rest of this page can claim. A single
snapshot of the inlet says what the hardware is doing at the moment you press the button. It does not
say what happened during the charge.

## The socket lock and the cable

The lock state is the trustworthy row. It decodes to unlocked or locked when the OBC reports one of
the two known codes, and to an unknown state carrying the raw byte otherwise. The screen labels the
unknown case as unknown instead of guessing that the plug is either in or out.

The cable rating is not trustworthy in the same way. The wire carries a 16-bit value, and the
encoding the OBC uses is not pinned to a capture. The decoder picks between a plain ampere reading
and a scaled, offset form, and it is explicitly experimental; the app labels the output as
unverified. Treat the number as a hint about the connected cable, not as a measurement.

## Pack voltage at the charger

The third row is the pack voltage the OBC sees at its terminals. The scaling on that identifier is
not pinned to a capture either, so the decoder is a heuristic: it chooses between three scalings from
the raw magnitude. A payload shorter than two bytes fails closed instead of returning a plausible
constant.

Read this row as a cross-reference against the pack voltage reported by the BECM. The two should
agree within a small margin when the car is connected and awake. A wide disagreement points at the
measurement, not at the pack. The BECM reading itself is covered in
[Battery diagnostics](/docs/battery-diagnostics).

## Diagnosing a charge limit

The diagnose button answers a different question: given that the car is charging slowly, which
constraint is the likely cause. It gathers six inputs in one pass:

| Input | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Actual charge power | The OBC's reported AC input power |
| Charging now | The OBC's own charging flag |
| Pack temperature | The warmest pack temperature channel |
| State of charge | The primary pack channel |
| Charge-limit ceiling | The pack's own charge power limit |
| Cable rating | The charging screen's cable row |

The rules run in a fixed order, and the first one whose evidence holds decides the answer:

1. **Battery temperature.** The pack is below 5 °C or above 45 °C while actual power is under
   7.0 kW. Cold and hot packs gate charge power on this platform.
2. **State of charge.** SoC is above 90 % while actual power is under 7.0 kW. The pack tapers by
   design near full.
3. **Cable capability.** The pilot rating implies a lower ceiling than the battery's own limit, and
   actual power sits at that cable ceiling. The arithmetic treats the rating as a 230 V single-phase
   supply, so the ceiling is the rating in amps × 0.23, with a 1 kW tolerance.
4. **On-board charger ceiling.** Actual power sits within 1 kW of an 11 kW charger ceiling.
5. **Not attributable.** No signal establishes a cause.

The order matters when evidence overlaps, because the earliest matching rule keeps the verdict. Each
answer prints the evidence sentence behind it, and the screen lists every input with its value or a
dash. The pack temperature, SoC and charge-limit inputs are the same channels the battery screens
read; the actual power and charging flag come from the OBC.

<Callout kind="caution" title="The thresholds are provisional heuristics">
The 5 °C and 45 °C window, the 7.0 kW floor, the 90 % taper point, the 230 V cable arithmetic and the
11 kW charger rating are the code's own provisional thresholds, not manufacturer specifications, and
they still need testing on more cars. The charger rating in particular is catalogue knowledge that
still needs testing on more cars. Read the verdict as a lead to check, not as a diagnosis of a fault.
</Callout>

## When the diagnosis says nothing

Two gates run before any rule. With no measured charge power there is nothing to explain, and the
answer is not attributable. If the OBC reports that it is not charging, there is likewise no
limitation to explain.

That is the honest outcome on a car where the OBC does not answer at all. The 2026-09-02 software
profile returned no usable payload for the OBC power read, and the actual-power input is taken from
that read. On such a car the diagnosis can only return not attributable, no matter what the
temperature and state of charge say. A missing input narrows the analysis; it is never filled with a
zero.

## What the app can and cannot see

What it can see:

- the AC inlet lock state, cable rating, and pack voltage at the charger
- the charge power the OBC reports, and its charging flag
- pack temperature, state of charge, and the pack's own charge-limit value

What it cannot see:

- DC fast charging, which is not read on this screen
- charge sessions, session history, or energy added
- the charge curve over time, or a target charge limit you set
- any cable or wallbox data beyond the pilot rating the car reports

## Charging and the inspection score

Charging readings are captured in the inspection report, but a charge limitation is a note, not a
score input. It cannot change the verdict. The four inputs that carry score weight are battery state
of health, the rested 12 V rail, fault codes, and odometer agreement. The rule behind the charger
ceiling is classed as a heuristic, which caps it at a note by construction. See
[Inspection reports](/docs/inspection-reports) for the full arithmetic.

The 12 V side of the same capture does score, through the rested rail voltage. That read is on the
power tab; see [Subsystem telemetry](/docs/subsystem-telemetry).
