## 1. Inspecting a used EV before buying it

When you have 10 minutes with a car on a test drive or at a dealership:

1. **Connect and run a full check**: Plug in your ENET cable, wake the dashboard, connect on the **Connection** screen, and click **Run full check** on **Overview**.
2. **Check the Battery Health screen**:
   - Look at **State of Health (SoH)** to see how much usable capacity remains compared with when the pack was new.
   - Check the **Cell Map** spread (delta in millivolts). A healthy, well-balanced pack typically shows all cell groups within a few millivolts of each other.
   - Check the **8-band temperature history** to see if the battery has spent unusual time at extreme temperatures.
3. **Check the Cross-Check screen**:
   - Verify that the **VIN** and **Odometer mileage** agree across the car's modules. If one module reports a different VIN or a much higher mileage than the dashboard, the app flags the disagreement immediately.
4. **Print or save the Inspection Report**:
   - Open **Inspection Report**, pick the **Executive inspection** or **Battery certificate** layout, and print or save it as a PDF. Your session is also saved automatically in **Sessions & Reports** so you can compare multiple used cars side by side at home.

## 2. Investigating a dashboard warning light

1. Connect to the car and open **Fault Codes (DTCs)**, then click **Scan all modules**.
2. Keep the filter on **Needs Attention** first. Modern cars store hundreds of internal self-test records that never light a warning lamp; **Needs Attention** shows only the **Active** and **Pending** faults that matter right now.
3. Click any fault code to open its detail drawer:
   - Read the plain-language description of the code and which module reported it.
   - Inspect the **Freeze-frame snapshot** to see the exact mileage, speed, 12 V battery voltage, and battery temperature at the moment the fault occurred. (A cascade of communication faults with a freeze-frame voltage below 11.5 V often points to a weak 12 V battery rather than a failed high-voltage component.)

## 3. Verifying a repair and clearing fault codes

1. **Before the repair**: Run a scan so your "before" state (including freeze-frame snapshots) is saved in **Sessions & Reports**.
2. **After completing the physical repair**: Connect to the car, open **Fault Codes (DTCs)**, and click **Clear fault codes**.
3. By default, Hanterill clears codes only in control units that reported faults. It saves available fault details first, then scans again to show which codes remain or return.
4. Click **Compare with previous scan** on the Fault Codes screen to view a side-by-side before-and-after record showing which codes cleared.

## 4. Checking what a dealer visit or OTA update changed

1. Before taking your car into a workshop (or before installing an over-the-air software update), run a scan so Hanterill saves your car's module software inventory.
2. When you get the car back, run a new scan and open **Sessions & Reports -> Firmware Diff**.
3. Hanterill compares every control unit side by side and highlights exactly which modules received new software versions and which ones were untouched.

## 5. Chasing an overnight 12 V battery drain

1. Connect to the car after it has been parked overnight and open **12 V Sleep Drain**.
2. Check the **12 V battery health, State of Charge, and standby current histogram** to see how many hours the car spent in deep sleep versus higher current-draw bands.
3. Check the **Wake-state history** and **TCAM & GPS** screens to see whether the telematics module or a body controller kept waking the car up while parked. See [Parasitic drain](/docs/parasitic-drain).
