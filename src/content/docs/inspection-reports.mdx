Most of Hanterill shows you raw readings and lets you decide. The inspection report is different:
it answers one question, "what condition is this car in?", with a score and a verdict.

Because that is a judgement rather than a measurement, the rules behind it are written down,
versioned, and shipped with the report itself. This page explains how they work.

<SafetyNotice level="read-only" title="Reads in, rules out">
Every input to the score comes from the read-only services described in [DTC scanning](/docs/dtc-scanning)
and [Battery diagnostics](/docs/battery-diagnostics). Scoring adds arithmetic, never traffic.
</SafetyNotice>

## The four verdicts

The report ends in one of four plain answers:

| Verdict | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Sound | No rule raised a concern and the score is high. |
| Advisories | Something worth knowing about, but nothing safety-critical. |
| Attention | A hard rule fired, or the score is low. Read the findings before you decide anything. |
| Inconclusive | Not enough answered to earn a verdict. |

Inconclusive is a real outcome, not a failure. If the battery health could not be read, the report
refuses to guess rather than scoring the car on partial evidence.

## The rule catalogue (v1.0.0)

Six groups of rules carry score weight:

| Rule group | What it looks at |
| --- | --- |
| Battery degradation | State of Health against published bands, including the manufacturer's warranty floor |
| Cell balance | Voltage spread across cell groups, with watch bands from published community research |
| 12 V support battery | The rested voltage of the low-voltage battery, which starts the car's electronics |
| 12 V quiescent draw | How much current the car draws while asleep, from its own measured history |
| Fault severity | Codes weighted by ISO 14229 status class and SAE J2012 code class, not counted flat |
| Odometer consistency | Mileage cross-read across modules; disagreement is its own finding |

Everything else in the report is a note: thermal spread, an overdue service interval, uptime
spread, a charge limitation, key count. Those describe what was seen and never move the score.

## Every rule names its evidence

Each rule cites one evidence class, and the class sets a ceiling on how strong a verdict that rule
can push:

| Class | Example | Strongest verdict it can force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Published standard | ISO status-bit meanings | Attention |
| Manufacturer spec | The 8-year / 160,000 km warranty floor | Attention |
| Vehicle capture | Measured rest spread and sleep draw from hardware sessions | Advisories |
| Community research | Cell-imbalance watch bands | Advisories |
| Heuristic | A charger ceiling guess | Note only, never affects the score |

A rule resting on a decoder that is still provisional is downgraded one class automatically. That
is why some findings read as observations rather than judgements: the tool reports what it does not
yet know.

## The two hard overrides

Two findings skip the arithmetic and force an Attention verdict on their own:

- A confirmed, currently failing fault in a safety system (airbags, ABS/ESP, steering angle, ADAS).
- Stored odometer copies that disagree beyond normal drift. That disagreement is the strongest
  mileage-fraud signal a used-car inspection can find, and the report states the magnitude of the
  disagreement, never an accusation.

## When inputs are missing

Missing inputs degrade the report toward inconclusive rather than toward a false all-clear:

- A channel that never answered is listed as a coverage gap. It cannot pass a check and cannot fail
  one.
- A DTC scan that did not reach every module marks the code evidence partial, and code rules then
  cannot push the verdict above advisories on their own.
- The report prints its own rule version, its coverage, and a SHA-256 checksum, so a later reading
  can be traced to the rules that produced it.
- Re-scoring a stored session under a newer rule set is a local calculation. No car needed.

## Thermal findings stay notes

The pack temperature sensors have no established mapping to individual modules yet, so thermal
spread is reported as an observation with zero effect on the score. It graduates to a real rule
only when the placement is corroborated by evidence.

## The score itself

The arithmetic is simple: start at 100, subtract each rule's points, floor at 0. The verdict then
follows the score, unless a rule forced a stronger one:

- 85 or above, with nothing forced upward: **sound**
- 60 or above, or any advisory rule fired: **advisories**
- below 60, or a hard override fired: **attention**

If the battery health could not be read, the score is left empty rather than guessed, and the
verdict is **inconclusive**.

## Location privacy

Pre-purchase inspection reports carry no GPS sections at all. Where other exports do include
coordinates, they are rounded to three decimals, roughly 110 metres. See [Privacy](/docs/privacy).

## In the app

- **Report page**: identity, battery, fault, ECU, service and thermal findings with the score.
- **Print**: the browser's own print pipeline with styled print CSS, so the paper copy matches the
  screen.
- **Sessions**: stored sessions re-score under current rules without reconnecting to a car.

The full design document, including every band and point value, lives in the app repository at
`docs/product/ppi-intelligence-and-scoring.md`.
