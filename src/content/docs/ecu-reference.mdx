A car is not one computer. It is dozens of small computers, called ECUs (electronic
control units), wired together. Each one runs something: the brakes, the windows, the
battery, the radio. Hanterill asks each of them what it is, what software it runs, and
whether anything is wrong.

This page lists every ECU Hanterill can talk to on the CMA platform, with the address
each one answers on.

## The registry

The CMA registry holds 49 entries. Forty of them advertise fault-code support. Forty-four
have been observed on a live car; the remaining five are `ref` rows: reference entries from
vendor documentation that have never answered. On a battery-electric car those five stay
quiet, and Hanterill records the silence as expected instead of a fault. In other words, a
BEV sees a roster of 44, while the 49-row list is the full CMA/ICE mapping.

Each entry carries a DoIP logical address. Think of it as a desk extension inside the
car: `0x1635` is always the battery controller, the same way 432 is always the same
desk. Hanterill sends its request to one extension, and that extension answers.

## Gateway

Two modules sit between your laptop and everything else. The VGM is the firewall, and
the CEM is the car's central body computer.

VGM writes need a security key that is unique per car. Reads work without one, so
every read-only feature in Hanterill runs on a connected vehicle without extra setup.

## Energy

This is the high-voltage side. The BECM manages the traction battery, the VCU decides
how much power the motors get, and the two inverters turn battery current into motor
current (and back, when you lift off).

<Callout kind="note" title="Why ECM is not an engine controller here">
On this platform the module called ECM is live-verified and is the drive-unit/inverter
controller, not an engine controller. The air-suspension module, SUM, is the reference row
people usually expect to find in its place. The name on the box is a label, not a
description; the registry says which modules actually answered.
</Callout>

## How Hanterill talks to them

Four UDS services do most of the work:

<SpecList rows={[
  ["0x22", "ReadDataByIdentifier: ask a module for a numbered value, such as its VIN (0xF190) or its software version (0xF195)"],
  ["0x19", "ReadDTCInformation: ask a module for its stored fault codes"],
  ["0x10", "DiagnosticSessionControl: ask a module to open a diagnostic session so it will answer the first two"],
  ["0x3E", "TesterPresent: a quiet \"still here\" so the car does not put modules to sleep mid-scan"],
]} />

The common identity identifiers, read from every module that answers:

<SpecList rows={[
  ["0xF190", "VIN"],
  ["0xF18C", "ECU serial number"],
  ["0xF193", "Supplier hardware version"],
  ["0xF194", "Supplier software number"],
  ["0xF195", "Supplier software version"],
]} />

Because Hanterill reads these from every ECU rather than only the battery, one scan
produces a full hardware and software inventory of the car. That is what the
[firmware view](/docs/firmware-and-inventory) diffs between visits.

## Other platforms keep their own registries

The list above is the CMA registry, the one Hanterill was built and checked against. SPA and
SEA1 carry their own, smaller registries. SPA has a separate CAN-era set and DoIP set, and
SEA1 has its own. A definition is never shared between platforms. A module that is present
on the reference car can be absent elsewhere, or answer at a different address, so each
platform is resolved on its own terms rather than by family resemblance.

## Not every module answers everything

Some modules answer identification reads but have no fault codes to report. Nine of the CMA
entries are in that state. Those are marked `DTCs: no` above, and Hanterill skips them during
a fault scan instead of recording a failure. A module that stays completely silent is a third
case: it is recorded as not observed, never as broken.

<Callout kind="note" title="A timeout is not a diagnosis">
When a module does not answer, it may not be fitted, may be asleep, or may not be on this
car's build list. Hanterill says "did not answer" and leaves the judgement to you.
</Callout>
