A car has many control units. Some manage the battery or brakes; others handle climate control, lights or doors. Hanterill checks which ones respond when you connect. This guide explains what the units do and why one may be absent from a scan.
The catalogue below names the control units Hanterill knows about on CMA cars. A listed unit is not necessarily fitted to every car.

## How the module registry works

The CMA module registry contains 49 control unit definitions:

- **44 modules** have been observed and verified on live battery-electric vehicles.
- **40 modules** support fault-code reporting.
- **5 modules** (`ref` entries) represent combustion, hybrid, all-wheel-drive couplings, or air-suspension controllers that are only fitted on specific powertrains or trim levels. On a pure battery-electric car without air suspension, those five modules stay quiet, and Hanterill records their silence as expected rather than flagging a fault.

Every control unit has a unique network address on the vehicle network. You can think of this
address like an office phone extension inside the car: one extension always rings the battery
management unit, another always rings the climate controller, and another always rings the
driver's door module. Hanterill sends its request to a specific extension, and that module
answers directly.

## Gateway modules

Two modules sit between your laptop and the rest of the vehicle. The **Vehicle Gateway Module
(VGM)** acts as the network router and firewall behind the diagnostic port, and the **Central
Electronic Module (CEM)** acts as the main body computer and security coordinator.

<EcuReference domain="gateway" />

Reading identification data, sensor values, and fault codes through the gateway works in
read-only mode without unlocking any security levels.

## Energy and propulsion modules

This group covers the high-voltage battery and electric drive system. The **Battery Energy
Control Module (BECM)** monitors the high-voltage pack and individual cell voltages, the
**Vehicle Computational Unit (VCU)** coordinates driver torque requests and driving modes, and
the front and rear **inverters (yarns/drive control modules)** convert DC battery power into
AC power for the electric motors and capture regenerative braking energy.

## How Hanterill communicates with each module

During a normal scan, Hanterill uses four standard diagnostic requests:

<SpecList rows={[
  ["Read data values", "Asks a module for a specific piece of information, such as its VIN, software version, or a live sensor reading."],
  ["Read fault codes", "Asks a module for its stored, pending, or permanent Diagnostic Trouble Codes (DTCs) and their status."],
  ["Open diagnostic session", "Places a module into its standard or extended diagnostic mode so its full set of sensors and identification records can be read."],
  ["Keep-alive heartbeat", "Sends a quiet periodic pulse while a scan is actively running so modules do not go back to sleep mid-read."],
]} />

From every module that answers, Hanterill reads a standard set of identity records:

<SpecList rows={[
  ["Vehicle Identification Number (VIN)", "Confirms the module belongs to the connected car and checks for mismatched replacement parts."],
  ["ECU serial number", "Identifies the physical circuit board installed in the vehicle."],
  ["Hardware part and version", "Shows the physical hardware revision reported by the supplier."],
  ["Software number and version", "Shows the exact firmware currently running on the module."],
]} />

Because Hanterill collects these identity records from every responding module across the car,
a single scan produces a complete hardware and software inventory that the
[Firmware & Inventory](/docs/firmware-and-inventory) screen can compare between service visits.

## Tools inside the ECU Registry screen

When you select an individual module in the **ECUs** screen inside the app, you can:

- **Inspect identity and fault counts**: See the module's full name, network domain, answered software/hardware versions, and current fault count.
- **Check security lock status (read-only)**: Query whether the module's protected maintenance levels are currently locked or unlocked without attempting to unlock them or changing anything on the car.
- **Reset a single module**: Send a targeted module restart command when you need to reboot one specific control unit after troubleshooting, behind an explicit confirmation dialog.

## Separate registries for SPA and SEA1

The table above shows the 49-module **CMA** registry. **SPA** (41 catalogued modules) and
**SEA1** (25 catalogued modules) each maintain their own independent module registries.
Hanterill never assumes a module address from one platform applies to another: each platform
uses its own verified address map.

## Why some modules stay silent

Not every module in the catalogue is fitted to every car:

- **No fault-code storage**: Nine entries in the CMA catalogue answer identification reads normally but do not store fault codes of their own (`DTCs: no`). Hanterill skips them during fault scans rather than reporting an error.
- **Optional equipment**: Modules for air suspension, rear seat heating, a towbar, or a head-up display only answer on cars built with those options.

<Callout kind="note" title="A silent module is not necessarily broken">
When a module does not answer, it may simply not be fitted to your car's trim level, or it may
be asleep because the car is not in active ignition mode. Hanterill reports "did not answer"
so you can distinguish an absent option from an actual communication fault.
</Callout>
