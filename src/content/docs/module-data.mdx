Most screens answer one question at a time: is the battery healthy, or why did the 12 V battery go flat over the weekend. The **Module data** screen is different: it shows every named group of readings a module keeps about itself, in one place. It is the closest thing in the app to reading the module's own service records.

## How a readout works

Each module exposes numbered *readout groups*: small sets of related records such as "charge history", "wake-up log" or "12 V statistics". On the **Module data** screen:

- A group **reads as soon as you open it**, and **Read again** in the group header refreshes it.
- A plain status line tells you how much of the read carried meaning, for example *"All 17 values read."* A group where nothing decoded says so in one line instead of showing filler.
- Key figures appear as stat tiles, followed by a table of the rows that have a plain value. Rows the app cannot yet decode stay folded away with the reason in words; the counts line still reports how much of the read was meaningful.
- Raw identifiers (DID numbers, payload bytes) never clutter the screen; they are kept in the saved JSON if you archive the session.

## What each module keeps

- **Battery module (BECM)**: lifetime charge counts (normal AC and fast DC, shown only when the charger's own tables agree), time spent in each charge band, per-cell class and capacity sets, temperature histograms by operating state, and the module's awake time.
- **Central module (CEM)**: 12 V battery statistics (charge counts, standby currents, temperature bands), the power-mode history (minutes asleep, parked, in use, driving), wake-up and sleep history, and the gateway reset log.
- **Gateway (VGM)**: security and diagnostic-access logs, network-management and Ethernet error histories, and the reset log with an odometer reading per reset.
- **Instrument cluster (DIM)**: trip computer, odometer copies, service intervals, display and system data, and the message history. See [Driving and display](/docs/driving-display).
- **On-board charger (OBC)**: charge cycles, restarts and lifetime alarms per power block.
- **Climate, thermal manager, drive units, engine module**: coolant circuits and pumps, heat-pump and heater lifetime figures, drive-unit operating hours and heat maps, and the OBD values the engine module mirrors.

Some groups also appear in place on the screen that owns the topic (the battery groups on **Battery Health**, the drive-unit groups on **Drive Units**), so you rarely need to leave the screen you are working in.

## Histories and timelines

Many groups are *histories*: dated events rather than single numbers. Each event is aged against the module's own clock, so a lock event reads as a real timestamp and a reset log entry carries the odometer at the time it happened. Structured events say what they were in words (a lock or unlock, a wake-up with its reason, a reset with its counter) instead of a bare code.

## What the screen will not invent

<Callout kind="caution" title="Readings keep their honest labels">
Values the app cannot yet decode stay undecoded, hours whose scale is unconfirmed are marked *likely*, and histogram bins without established edges are numbered rather than labelled with units. A verdict you can act on says so; everything else says what is missing.
</Callout>

Some groups need the module's extended diagnostic session; those carry an **extended** toggle in the group header and read once you allow it.

## Where the data goes

Every readout is part of the session archive, so a capture saved today can be compared with a later visit. See [Sessions and evidence](/docs/sessions-and-evidence) for how sessions, exports and the "Since your last visit" alerts work.
