Even on an electric car with a huge high-voltage battery in the floor, **every computer, door lock, relay, and safety system runs on the small 12 V lead-acid battery** under the hood. When you park and lock the car, the high-voltage battery disconnects for safety until you next drive or charge. If something keeps the car's electronics awake overnight, that small 12 V battery can run flat, leaving the car unable to unlock or close its high-voltage contactors in the morning.

The **12 V Sleep Drain** screen reads the **Battery Monitoring Sensor (BMS)** on the 12 V battery terminal and the **Central Electronic Module (CEM)** so you can see how healthy your 12 V battery is and whether the car is actually sleeping when parked.

## 1. Why reading live current while plugged in is not enough

As soon as you open the driver's door, sit in the seat, and plug in a diagnostic cable, the car wakes up its displays, gateway, and control units. Live 12 V current draw while the car is awake is typically **10 to 25 Amps**, which tells you nothing about what the car was doing at 3:00 AM while parked and locked.

Fortunately, the car's **Battery Monitoring Sensor** stays awake 24 hours a day and keeps a continuous internal log of how much current the car drew while parked and locked. Hanterill reads that stored history after you wake the car up.

## 2. What the 12 V Sleep Drain screen shows

### Current 12 V battery health and charge
- **12 V State of Charge (%) and State of Health (%)**: How full the 12 V AGM battery is right now and how much of its original capacity remains.
- **12 V voltage, current, and temperature**: Measured directly at the battery post by the monitoring sensor, plus cross-checks against the 12 V supply voltage seen by other modules in the car.
- **Days in service and lifetime energy counters**: How long the current 12 V battery adaptation has been running and how much total charge has flowed in and out of it.

### Parked standby current histogram (time in band)
A healthy, sleeping car draws a tiny trickle of current (typically **under 20 to 50 milliamps**, or `0.02` to `0.05 A`) to keep the keyless entry receiver and alarm listening.

Hanterill reads the car's **standby current histogram**, which records how many hours and minutes the parked car spent in each current-draw band:
- **Deep sleep band (0 to 50 mA)**: Normal parked sleep.
- **Light wake / background check bands (50 mA to 1 A)**: Brief wake-ups (for example, checking cellular connection or battery status).
- **Elevated drain bands (1 A and above)**: A module or accessory (such as a stuck telematics modem, an aftermarket dashcam wired to permanent power, or a door latch that never signaled locked) is preventing the car from going to sleep.

### Accumulated sleep energy draw and wake reasons
Alongside the time-in-band histogram, Hanterill shows the **total amp-hours (Ah) drawn while parked** and the **gateway wake-state log**, helping you see whether the **Telematics module (TCAM)**, a charger handshake, or a body module repeatedly woke the vehicle overnight.

## 3. How to investigate a flat or weak 12 V battery

1. Leave the car locked overnight without plugging in a charger.
2. The next morning, connect Hanterill and open **12 V Sleep Drain** right away.
3. Check whether the parked hours accumulated in the **0 to 50 mA** deep-sleep band or in the higher current bands.
4. If the car slept normally all night (under 50 mA) but the 12 V State of Charge still dropped sharply, the 12 V battery itself is worn out and losing charge internally. If you replace the 12 V battery, remember to run the **12 V battery adaptation reset** in [Service functions](/docs/service-functions) so the car knows a fresh battery is installed.
