A parked car is never fully off. Its computers keep a small current flowing out of the 12 V
battery, and when one of them fails to sleep that current stays high. Hanterill reads the CEM's own
record of that current and shows how it is distributed over time, not just one number.

<SafetyNotice level="read-only">
Everything on this page is a read. Hanterill asks the CEM for two data identifiers and decodes the
answers. It does not write to the module, and it does not change how the car sleeps.
</SafetyNotice>

## What quiescent current is

Quiescent current is the current a car draws from its 12 V battery once it has gone to sleep, in
milliamps. A healthy car settles to a low figure and holds it. A module that keeps waking, or that
never sleeps at all, holds the figure up and flattens the battery over days of parking.

Hanterill does not clamp an ammeter to the battery. The CEM (Central Electronic Module) already
records the measurement, and Hanterill reads that record over `0x22 ReadDataByIdentifier`.

## The two records the CEM holds

| DID | What it holds | Wire format |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0x4025` | The average quiescent current in mA | 2 bytes, big-endian signed, in tenths of a milliamp |
| `0x40D7` | How past sleep samples fell into current bands | 7 counts, 2 bytes each, at least 14 bytes |

The average is signed: a negative value means current leaving the battery, which is the usual
sense. A raw value of `0x012C` is 300 tenths, displayed as **30.0 mA**.

The histogram read is required. If the CEM does not answer it, the whole read fails with a typed
error and the screen stays empty. The average is optional: if that read fails, the average is left
empty and the status says the standby current was not reported. Hanterill never fills it with zero.

<Callout kind="note" title="No CEM mapping, no read">
On a platform where Hanterill has no address for the CEM, the drain read is not attempted at all.
It reports that the feature does not apply rather than reading a guessed address. A CEM that
rejects the histogram identifier is reported as unsupported.
</Callout>

## The sleep-current histogram

The histogram is a record of past sleep samples, grouped into seven current bands. The bands are
fixed by the decoder:

| Band | Samples counted |
| --- | --- |
| `0–9 mA` | sleep current below 10 mA |
| `10–19 mA` | |
| `20–29 mA` | |
| `30–39 mA` | |
| `40–79 mA` | one wide band |
| `80–119 mA` | one wide band |
| `120+ mA` | open-ended, with 255 mA as the conventional ceiling |

The screen draws the seven counts as bars on a square-root scale, prints the sample count above
each bar, and draws a band with no samples as an honest zero. Under the bars sits a table with the
same numbers: samples and share of the total. The histogram is a count of samples, not a current
reading, so a tall bar means "often", not "much".

## What the bands mean

Hanterill colours the bands and labels them for a reader:

- **0–39 mA** is normal. The screen states that normal OEM sleep stays below 50 mA.
- **40–119 mA** is elevated: a module is likely waking periodically.
- **120 mA and above** is critical: at that level the car never slept.

The screen names the highest band that recorded at least one sample and says what its class means.
It also offers a next step that follows the reading: the service schedule for a normal result,
fault codes and the TCAM module for an elevated one, fault codes for a critical one.

<Callout kind="caution" title="The backend classifies nothing">
The drain report carries a descriptive status only. It reads either "Measured standby current, no
diagnostic policy applied" or "Standby current not reported". The band colours, the class words and
the next-step line are the screen's reading aids, not a versioned diagnostic policy. Treat the
histogram as measurement evidence and the class as interpretation.
</Callout>

## The average against the histogram

The average (`0x4025`) and the histogram (`0x40D7`) are independent reads, so they can disagree. To
catch a gross disagreement, Hanterill works out an implied mean from the histogram by weighting
each band count with that band's midpoint, then compares it with the reported average.

- The tolerance is the larger of **15 mA** and **0.75 times the larger of the two means**.
- Inside the tolerance, the two agree.
- Outside it, the screen raises an "Inconsistent readings" banner with both figures and the sample
  count. One of the two reads is wrong, and showing them as a quiet pair would hide that.

The midpoints stand in for a distribution the histogram does not carry, so the check is a tripwire,
never a verdict. The open-ended `120+ mA` band uses 255 mA as its midpoint, the same conventional
ceiling the bands use.

## What keeps a car awake

A drain that never falls can come from a module that never sleeps. Module uptime counters are the
clue: Hanterill reads DID `0xDD00` from the master modules, where each counter is four bytes in
tenths of a second since that module last started.

The counters are compared with each other. When at least two modules answered and their uptimes
agree within **one hour**, the app treats the counters as having started together, so none of those
modules has been reset or suspended since. A large agreed uptime therefore means the master modules
have been continuously powered for that whole span. That is a useful clue when hunting a drain, and
it is not proof on its own: one module can still hold the bus awake while the others sleep normally.

## Finding modules that stay quiet

The other direction matters too, and a normal scan can miss it. Some fitted modules do not answer a
plain sweep, so they either need the quiet-module cascade or they need a closer look. That sweep is
described in [Captures and sweeps](/docs/captures-and-sweeps). On the drain side, the question it
helps with is simple: if a module answers when the car should be asleep, it is a candidate for the
current the histogram shows.

## The battery page and the inspection score

The drain screen and the battery screen read different computers and answer different questions.
The battery page reads the BECM and covers state of health, state of charge and cell balance. The
drain screen reads the CEM and covers the 12 V sleep current. The battery page links across to the
drain screen, and the drain histogram travels into the health capture and is embedded in the
inspection report as its own section.

No drain number is scored. The inspection score uses two battery inputs only: state of health and
the rested 12 V rail from the BECM. A rail below 12.2 V deducts 16 points, and 12.2 to 12.5 V
deducts 6. The quiescent average and the histogram are carried as observations and are not part of
the arithmetic. The full policy is in [Inspection reports](/docs/inspection-reports).
