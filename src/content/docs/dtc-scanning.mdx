The car's control units check their sensors and connections as you drive. When one detects a problem, it may save a fault code. **Fault Codes** shows the codes reported by each responding control unit and, when available, the conditions the car saved with them.

## 1. Why the screen opens on "Needs Attention"

If you ask a modern Volvo or Polestar for every status record across all 40+ fault-capable modules without filtering, the car will return **hundreds of internal records**. That is because the diagnostic standard uses the same table both for real faults and for tracking whether hundreds of routine self-tests have finished running since the last time codes were cleared.

To keep you from scrolling through hundreds of normal self-test entries, Hanterill decodes each code's status flags into four clear categories and opens the **Fault Codes** screen filtered to **Needs Attention** by default:

| Status badge | What it means in plain language | Shown in "Needs Attention"? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Active** | The fault is happening **right now** at the moment of the scan. | **Yes** |
| **Pending** | The module saw the problem during the current or previous drive cycle and is watching to see if it happens again before lighting a warning lamp. | **Yes** |
| **Stored** | The fault happened enough times in the past to be confirmed and saved in memory, even if it is not happening at this exact second. | Shown under **Stored / All** |
| **Historical** | An old or cleared event kept only in the module's long-term log. | Shown under **All** |

You can switch the filter bar between **Needs Attention**, **Active**, **Pending**, **Stored**, and **All** at any time, or filter by module domain (Powertrain, Chassis, Body, Network).

## 2. How to read a fault code

Automotive fault codes follow a standard 7-character format such as `P0A80-00` or `B1B25-13`:

- **First letter (the system)**:
  - **P** (*Powertrain*): motors, inverters, high-voltage battery, charger, transmission, or engine.
  - **C** (*Chassis*): brakes, stability control, steering, or suspension.
  - **B** (*Body*): climate control, airbags, lighting, doors, seats, or instrument cluster.
  - **U** (*Network / Communication*): a missing message or low-voltage dip between two modules on the car's network.
- **Middle characters**: identify the specific sensor, circuit, or component.
- **Last two characters (the failure symptom)**: describe *how* the circuit failed, such as an open wire, a short to ground, a signal out of range, or a missing calibration.

## 3. Freeze-frame snapshots: what the car was doing when the fault happened

A fault code by itself tells you *what* complained, but the **Freeze-frame snapshot** tells you *when and under what conditions* it happened.

When you click any fault code in Hanterill, the detail drawer fetches the module's stored snapshot and extended counters:
- **Odometer mileage** when the fault was first and last recorded.
- **12 V system voltage** at the moment the fault tripped. (If five modules logged communication codes at the exact same mileage and the freeze frame shows `10.8 V`, the real culprit was a weak 12 V battery during startup, not five broken modules.)
- **Vehicle speed, battery State of Charge, pack voltage, current, and temperatures** at the second the fault set.
- **Occurrence and healing counters**: how many times the fault has happened and how many clean drive cycles have passed since.

## 4. Comparing with your previous scan

At the top of the **Fault Codes** screen, clicking **Compare with previous scan** immediately compares your current scan against the most recent saved scan for the same car. It highlights:
- **New faults** that appeared since your last check.
- **Cleared faults** that disappeared after a repair.
- **Status changes** (for example, an **Active** fault that stepped down to **Stored**).

## 5. Offline fault-code lookup

**Code Lookup** works without a car connected. Enter a fault code to see its description, system and reported failure type when the app has a matching entry.

## 6. Clearing fault codes safely

Clearing fault codes is a vehicle-changing action, so it always asks for your explicit confirmation first. When you click **Clear fault codes**, Hanterill gives you clear, practical options:

- **Clear faulted modules only (Default) or Clear all modules**: By default, Hanterill only sends the clear command to the modules that actually reported fault codes, which finishes much faster and avoids resetting clean modules unnecessarily. You can switch to **All modules** whenever you want a full-car clear.
- **Save freeze-frame snapshots before clearing (On by default)**: Because clearing a module's fault memory erases its stored freeze frames, Hanterill automatically saves those snapshots into your local session archive before erasing them.
- **Re-read and verify after clearing (On by default)**: After clearing, Hanterill pauses briefly and re-scans the modules to verify which codes stayed gone and which codes came right back because the underlying physical fault is still present.
- **Optional module restart for stubborn latched codes**: A few modules latch certain historical codes until the module restarts. When needed, and only while the car is safely parked, an optional reset-and-recheck step can restart stubborn modules and re-establish the diagnostic connection automatically.
